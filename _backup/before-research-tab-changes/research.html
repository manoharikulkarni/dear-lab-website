Research

Exploring the frontiers of RNA biology, disease engineering, and advanced sensing technologies

Overview

Decoding Non-Coding RNAs & Disease Biomechanics

At the DEAR Lab (Disease Engineering and Advanced RNA Biology Lab), our research investigates the fundamental and translational aspects of non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs, miRNAs) at the crossroads of cancer biology, mechanobiology, and cutting-edge sensing technologies. We combine sophisticated 3D glioblastoma tumoroid systems with quantum-based biosensors and innovative chemical modulators to illuminate cellular mechanics and decipher gene regulatory networks in lethal malignancies.

Core Pillar 01

lncRNA & miRNA Biology

Our group focuses on unravelling the biological functions and secondary structures of long non-coding RNAs—predominantly MALAT1 and NEAT1—as well as regulatory microRNAs including miR-21, miR-149, and miR-211. We delve deeply into the conformation and stability of higher-order G-quadruplex structures embedded within lncRNAs and delineate their dynamic interactome with vital RNA-binding proteins such as nucleolin and nucleophosmin. In parallel, we engineer branched DNA nanostructures to specifically sequester and silence oncogenic miRNAs, shedding light on core miRNA biogenesis pathways and post-transcriptional networks.

MALAT1 NEAT1 miR-21 miR-149 G-quadruplex Nucleolin Branched DNA
Core Pillar 02

Mechanosensing

Mechanical forces originating in the tumor microenvironment exert profound control over cellular behavior and oncogenesis. We investigate how physical cues, notably altered extracellular matrix rigidity and compressive stress, modulate lncRNA transcription and drive invasive cancer progression. Our work highlights the mechanosensing role of the nuclear-retained oncogenic lncRNA NEAT1 on biomimetic hydrogel substrates of tuneable stiffness. By elucidating how glioblastoma (GBM) cells transduce physical strain into long-term gene regulatory adaptations, we reveal mechanical vulnerabilities amenable to clinical targeting.

NEAT1 Substrate Stiffness GBM Tumor Microenvironment Mechanotransduction
Core Pillar 03

Disease Modelling — Glioblastoma (GBM)

Traditional flat plastic culture systems fail to capture the complex architectural, biochemical, and biophysical cues encountered by brain tumor cells in vivo. We pioneer the engineering of biomimetic 3D tumoroid models that accurately reproduce the glioblastoma (GBM) microenvironmental niche. Through systematic comparative profiling of oncogenic lncRNAs NEAT1 and MALAT1 expression across 2D monolayers and 3D tumoroid platforms, we uncover how three-dimensional geometry reshapes RNA paraspeckle assembly, stemness maintenance, chemoresistance, and metastatic aggressiveness.

Glioblastoma 3D Tumoroids NEAT1 MALAT1 Cancer Progression Disease Models
Core Pillar 04

RNA Sensing Technologies / Quantum Sensing

Bridging solid-state quantum physics and molecular oncology, we explore the use of optically active nitrogen-vacancy (NV) center-bearing nanodiamonds as nanoscale quantum biosensors inside living cells. We employ quantum relaxometry and spin coherence to monitor chemotherapy-induced intracellular oxidative stress and free radical production in real time within glioblastoma cells. This platform enables us to establish next-generation quantum-assisted sensing tools that detect subtle biological perturbations, microenvironmental shifts, and RNA dynamics with unprecedented precision.

Quantum Sensors NV Centers Nano Diamonds Oxidative Stress Biosensing GBM
Core Pillar 05

Small Molecule Therapeutics

We design, synthesize, and screen targeted small molecules and synthetic conjugates capable of selectively modulating oncogenic non-coding RNAs. Our research demonstrates peptidic aminosugar-mediated interference in primary and precursor miR-21 biogenesis, which effectively reverses the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and re-establishes expression of the PTEN tumor suppressor. Furthermore, we develop dual-affinity peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) directed against oncogenic hairpin loops and harness quadruplex-binding ligands such as TmPyP4 to induce structural unfolding and restore critical anti-tumor regulatory cascades.
